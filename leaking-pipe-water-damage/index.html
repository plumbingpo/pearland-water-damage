Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Pearland

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. For Pearland homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. This is where our team comes in. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the Water SupplyWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Check the Water MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Shifting soil or abrasion wearing through water lines that run beneath a concrete slab.
  • Failed solder joints or crimp rings hidden inside wall and ceiling cavities.
  • Pipes in exterior walls or crawl spaces that crack after a freeze and seep once they thaw.
  • Fasteners from remodeling work or wall hangings that nick or pierce a line inside the wall.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Flooring Damage

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Wet Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

A pipe leaking above a ceiling or inside a wall leaves brown rings and bubbled paint. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Swollen Cabinets

Under a sink, a cabinet base made of particleboard puffs up and falls apart after it takes on water. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Insurance

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Coverage is common for accidental, sudden damage, while a leak that ran for months or weeks is usually not covered and is specifically excluded in some policies. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Read your policy and talk to your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • How long the pipe leaked and how far the moisture spread from it.
  • Access to the pipe, with a sink cabinet being cheaper to work in than a slab or a closed wall.
  • The number of rooms and floors affected, such as a ceiling underneath the leaking line.
  • How much drywall, insulation, cabinetry or flooring has to be removed and replaced.

How We Fix It

Our Leaking Pipe Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using a thermal camera and moisture meters, our crew checks cabinets, floors and walls and marks each wet spot, even those far from the pipe that water reached by running along the framing.

2

Make Targeted Openings

We make targeted openings to reach wet cavities, remove drywall, insulation or cabinet parts that cannot be saved, and bag any moldy material so spores are not spread through the house.

3

Structural Drying

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. Readings are taken at each visit, and the setup is changed as the materials dry.

4

Treat and Document

We apply antimicrobial treatment to affected surfaces and give you moisture logs and photos. Those records show the area was dry before repairs and help with any claim.
