What Usually Goes Wrong in San Antonio Homes

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in San Antonio, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: The top of the house takes on water when shingles are damaged or the seal around a vent pipe gives out. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in San Antonio, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. We pump out the spill and test the surrounding floor and wall bases, since water tends to run under them.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in San Antonio.

The First Hour After Water Damage in San Antonio

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. These are the mistakes our crews see most often in San Antonio, and what to do instead.

Never run an ordinary vacuum cleaner over a wet floor. You risk a shock and a dead vacuum, and a mop with old towels does the job safely. Do not turn on ceiling fans or lights under a wet ceiling, and stay out of rooms where the ceiling is sagging. Do not lift tacked-down carpet yourself, since it can shrink or tear and may be saveable if it is handled properly. Do not turn the heat up high to dry things out, because warm, damp air is what mold likes.

The helpful things are simple: close the valve, prop open cupboards and wardrobes, and stand wet cushions and textiles up to drain. Do take pictures before moving anything. Do keep children and pets away from the area. Most of all, phone early, so a Pearland Restoration crew is already heading for San Antonio while you work through this list.

How Drying Works on a San Antonio Water Damage Job

Drying a building is not the same as waiting for it to dry. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every San Antonio property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Then air movers are set along the walls to push a fast stream of air across wet surfaces and lift moisture out of them. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Without the dehumidifiers, the air movers would just move damp air around.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most San Antonio homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.
