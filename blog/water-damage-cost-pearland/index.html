How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Pearland?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Pearland?

The Short Answer

Typical Pearland water damage projects cost from $1,500 up to $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. Major flooding or a sewage backup that needs rebuilding can run well above $10,000.

These are typical national ranges, not a quote. Pricing in Pearland, TX depends on local wages and the going rate for materials, so your number may differ. An exact price requires someone to inspect the home in person and take moisture readings.

How Water Type Affects Cost

The first thing a restoration estimate looks at is what kind of water it is. The industry sorts water into three categories based on how contaminated it is. The dirtier the water, the more protective gear, cleaning and material removal the job needs.

Nationally, the category puts cleanup at about $3 to $7.50 for each square foot. Typical figures look like this.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterSupply lines, faucets, water heater tanksAbout $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterFailed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3, black waterStorm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

The category is not fixed. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. Putting things off raises the price for this reason, among others.

The Main Price Factors

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. When a job is priced, the contractor considers the following.

  • Area affected. A larger wet area takes extra crew hours, additional machines and a longer stay.
  • Water category. Jobs with clean water cost the least. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. Water spreads into subfloors and wall cavities over time. A leak that runs all weekend while you are gone costs far more than one caught in an hour.
  • Materials. Tile and concrete dry well. Special drying or outright replacement is common for drywall, hardwood, insulation and carpet pad.
  • Mold. Once mold is growing, the scope expands to include remediation, along with air filtration and containment.
  • Reconstruction. The drying phase has its own price. Putting back cabinets, paint, flooring and drywall is billed separately, and for a big loss that part is frequently larger.

Most estimates are built around two phases: water extraction first, then structural drying. Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days.

Many companies charge for each day the air movers and dehumidifiers run. Three days of drying is cheaper than six.

Typical Costs for Common Jobs

For the kinds of losses that come up most frequently in Pearland, national figures usually look like this. Treat them as a starting point, not a price.

  • Burst pipe. Drying and cleanup frequently total $1,500 to $5,000, and the burst pipe repair is on top of that. Expect more if it ran for hours or soaked more than one floor.
  • Appliance leak. Catch a leaking water heater, washing machine or dishwasher early and the bill is often $1,000 to $4,000. Slow leaks under cabinets cost more because of hidden damage.
  • Basement flood. The usual range is $2,000 to $10,000 or more, varying with the water's source, its depth and any finished surfaces. Costs are highest for basement water removal when the space is finished.
  • Sewage backup. Many jobs cost $3,000 to $10,000, some more. Anything porous has to go and all surfaces get disinfected, since cleaning up sewage counts as Category 3.
  • Roof leak. Interior work on wet ceilings, walls and insulation often comes to $1,000 to $5,000, and the roof fix is extra.

Is Water Damage in Pearland Covered by Insurance?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

A leak that developed slowly is usually excluded. An insurer may treat a fitting that dripped for months as a maintenance problem. Flood water from outside, such as a rising river or storm surge, is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood insurance. A backed-up sewer often takes an added endorsement.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. Remember to consider your deductible. On a small job, the repair may cost less than the deductible, and a claim may not make sense.

How to Keep the Bill Down

Most of the cost of water damage is decided in the first day or two. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water left in place goes on spreading. More material gets wet with each passing hour.
  • Shut off the water. Learn the location of the main valve ahead of time, and turn it off the moment a leak shows up.
  • Document everything. Take photos and video before anything is moved or thrown out. Save every receipt.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. It can take as little as 24 to 48 hours for mold to start. Once it does, the job gets longer and more expensive.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. If the water came from outside, leave flood cleanup to a crew with the right protective gear.

Turning a Range Into an Estimate

Averages from across the country are fine for planning and still cannot describe your home. To learn the true spread of the water, and the work required to dry and fix it, a technician has to inspect with a meter.
